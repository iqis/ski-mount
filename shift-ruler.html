Ski Mount Designer Shift Ruler Patterns
Ski mounting

Shift Ruler

A paper rule for moving a mount off the ski's recommended boot center. Centimetres on both long edges, graduated in millimetres like the mounting template, and a vertical scale on the 0 line long enough to reach the edges of the ski.

Companion to the Ski Mount Hole Designer, which prints the matching template and drill list.

One card, actual size

On screen this is close to true size; the PDF is the exact one. Print at Actual size (100%) and measure the 100 mm bar on the sheet before you cut.

How to use it

  1. Cut one card out along its border, then cut out the diamond at 0.
  2. Lay it on the ski with the diamond over the recommended boot center mark and the numbers running the way you want to move the mount — toward the tip to go forward, toward the tail to go back.
  3. Square it up on the vertical scale: read where each ski edge crosses it. Equal readings mean the card is centred and square, and twice the reading is the ski's width there.
  4. Mark the ski at the centimetre you want, against the edge of the card. That mark is the new boot center: line the mounting template's diamond up with it.

Reading the scale

Ticks are 1 mm, longer at 5 mm, longest and numbered at every centimetre. Both long edges carry the same scale, so one is always next to the mark you are making, and every tick runs right to the cut edge.

The card runs one way only. Turn it end for end to shift the mount the other way — the numbers read the same, the direction follows the ski.

If the print comes out small

Print dialogs shrink pages to the printer's own printable area — Chrome's Default scale does exactly that, and a Letter PDF sent to A4 paper (or the reverse) shrinks too. Pick Actual size or Custom 100%, and set the printer's paper to the size you chose here. If it still comes out small, measure the printed 100 mm bar, type that number in Printed 100 mm bar, and the PDF is redrawn that much bigger to cancel it.

The vertical scale reads out from the ski's centerline in millimetres, half-millimetre ticks within the first centimetre, numbered every centimetre. A 120 mm card reaches the edges of a 110 mm ski; on a narrower ski its long edges overhang, so pick 90 mm if you want them to land on the topsheet.

Mount Hole Designer

Lay several bindings out on one ski and see which holes can share an insert, which sit too close to drill, and where each boot centre lands.

  • 113 patterns, or type your own hole coordinates
  • Ladder positions: step a binding fore and aft in fixed increments
  • Shared-insert and spacing analysis, with a drill list
  • Tiled 1:1 PDF template with check bars and a cut-out diamond
  • Adapter plate modeller: STL, DXF and a design file for CAD

Shift Ruler

A paper rule for moving a mount off the ski's recommended boot centre. Cut one out, line the diamond up with the factory mark, and mark the new centre.

  • Centimetres on both long edges, graduated in millimetres
  • A vertical scale that reaches the edges of the ski, so the card sits square
  • 0 to 6, 4 or 12 cm; Letter, A4, Legal, Tabloid or A3
  • Printer calibration that cancels a print dialog's shrink

Where the numbers come from

Most were read out of the vector geometry of maker and shop templates rather than copied from a table: hole circles, boot-edge lines and heel adjustment ranges, measured directly in the PDF. Each entry carries what it was measured from and how well the sources agree.

Where sources disagree

They do, by a few millimetres, and the library says so instead of picking silently. Sliding heels vary the most between sources — often that is the binding's own adjustment range, not an error.

Test-mount before you drill

These are planning numbers, not a jig. Print at actual size, check the 100 mm bar, tape the template down and offer the binding up to it before a bit touches the ski. Drilling is permanent; a wrong pattern is a ruined topsheet at best.
